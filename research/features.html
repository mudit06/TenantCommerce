What a manufacturer's storefront needs

We studied how Indian and global manufacturers in sanitary ware, locks, door and furniture hardware, home decor and clothing run their websites, plus the checkout norms of Indian D2C brands. Every feature found is mapped below to a vendor industry, a build phase and the switch that turns it on for a vendor.

Key findings

Six patterns showed up across almost every site. They shape the data model more than the page designs do.

The website is a catalogue and service desk first

Many manufacturers sell mostly through dealers or marketplaces. Their sites exist to help people choose, find a dealer and get support. Jaquar sends online buyers to its Amazon store; Dorset also sells through Amazon and IndustryBuying.

Seen at Jaquar, Dorset, Hindware, Häfele

Technical data closes the sale

Buyers of fittings, locks and hardware compare finishes, dimensions in mm, door thickness and model numbers. Spec sheets, installation manuals and videos sit on the product page, and search works on article numbers.

Seen at CERA, Kohler, Häfele, Godrej

Dealers matter twice

Shoppers use dealer and store locators (Häfele lists 235 locations in India, Fabindia 340+ stores). Trade buyers need their own portal with dealer prices, volume tiers, quick order by SKU and credit limits.

Seen at Häfele, Fabindia, Pepperfry, B2B portal guides

After-sales is a feature set, not a page

Warranty registration with serial number and invoice, service requests with tracking, and spare-parts finders with exploded diagrams are standard for fittings and locks. GROHE extends warranty for products registered online.

Seen at GROHE, CERA, Kohler, Jaquar, Godrej

Indian checkout has its own rules

UPI and cash on delivery are expected, pincode serviceability is checked before the cart, invoices carry GSTIN and HSN codes, and WhatsApp is the support channel. COD is costly, so brands charge a COD fee or reward prepaid orders.

Seen at Snitch, Indian D2C guides (Fynd, Shopaccino)

Clothing runs on size and returns

Size guides, AI size suggestions and self-serve exchanges reduce the return rate, and drops and daily deals drive repeat visits. Return windows range from 7 days (Snitch) to 30 days (The Souled Store).

Seen at Fabindia, Snitch, The Souled Store, Bewakoof

Buyers compare finishes and dimensions, check that a fitting suits their plumbing, and often buy through a plumber, dealer or for a whole project.

  • Festival schemes, coupons, reviews and wishlist

What changes in the build

The research is already reflected in the instruction docs in this folder.

  • Products carry structured data from day oneAttribute sets per category, model numbers, documents, videos, warranty months and linked spare parts. See docs/06-data-model.md.
  • Growth features ship in the MVPSince 3 October 2026 festival schemes, launch offers, coupons, affiliates, reviews, wishlist, offer messages and abandoned cart reminders are in Phase 1 next to enquiries and dealers. Trade accounts for dealers, retailers, wholesalers and interior designers, trade schemes, loyalty points, comparison, warranty and spare parts follow in Phase 2. See docs/17-roadmap.md.
  • Every feature is a switch per vendorEach row in the matrix maps to a flag key or to the core, and the vendor's plan caps what can be switched on. See docs/08-modules-and-feature-flags.md.
  • Industry presets speed up onboardingCreating a sanitary store pre-selects sanitary flags, a clothing store clothing flags.
  • India checkout rules are built inPincode serviceability, COD fee and limits, prepaid discount, CGST/SGST or IGST split and GST invoice numbering. See docs/11-orders-payments-gst.md.
  • Shoppers hear from the vendor, never from usOrder updates go out on WhatsApp and SMS from each vendor's own number and sender ID, set up during onboarding, at about ₹1 per order on the vendor's own bills. See docs/18-notifications.md.

Method and limits

Jaquar, CERA, Hindware, Kohler, Godrej Security, Pepperfry and Häfele were checked on 1 October 2026. Häfele India, GROHE, Dorset, Yale India, Asian Paints, Fabindia, Snitch, The Souled Store and Bewakoof, plus Indian D2C and B2B dealer-portal guides, were checked through web search on 2 October 2026.

The second round relied on official pages and listings surfaced by search, not full walkthroughs of each checkout. Häfele's product pages could not be read in the first round. Treat the brand notes as what we observed, and recheck a brand before quoting it to a vendor.

WhatsApp and SMS rules and prices (Meta's developer docs, TRAI's DLT rules, MSG91 and Shiprocket help pages) and Shiprocket's API rules were checked on 2 October 2026. Meta changes its rate cards often, so recheck prices before quoting them.
